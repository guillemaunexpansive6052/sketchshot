## 🎬 How to Create a Storyboard

The interface features a main view area. You select camera settings on the left sidebar. You drag and drop elements into the frame.

### Setting the Frame
Start by picking an aspect ratio. Common choices include 16:9 for widescreen or 2.39:1 for theatrical cinema.

### Choosing Lenses
The lens menu changes the perspective. Use a wide-angle lens for environmental shots. Use a telephoto lens for portraits. The software updates the view in real-time. This shows exactly what your camera sees.

### Adding Elements
Place actors and objects into the scene. You move these items with your mouse. Adjust the scale to match your vision. You control the depth and distance of each subject.

## 🛠️ Advanced Settings

You find extra tools in the Settings menu.

- **Grid Overlays:** Turn on the rule of thirds. This helps you balance the frame.
- **Save Projects:** Your storyboards save as project files. You open these again to edit your work.
- **Export:** Save your boards as images. You print these or share them with your film crew.

## 💡 Best Practices for Filmmakers

Plan your coverage before you start. Create a wide shot to show the room. Create a medium shot to show the characters. Create a close-up to show emotions. Use the lens library to maintain consistency between shots. 

Consistency makes editing easier later. If you change a lens in one shot, remember to check your previous shots. This creates a smooth flow in your final movie.
